How to Detect AI-Generated Content

The statistical signals detectors look for, how to check AI-written work in law, finance and education, and where automated scores fall short.

In this article

  1. The Technical Mechanics of AI Detection
  2. Industry-Specific Detection Strategies
  3. Words of Caution: The Limits of Automated Detectors

As generative AI tools become embedded in modern business infrastructure, the challenge of identifying reports that have been made by AI moved from an academic curiosity to an operational priority.

Whether reviewing a quarterly report, analyzing a court filing, or evaluating a student’s thesis, organizations need reliable ways to distinguish human authorship from automated outputs.

Detecting AI-generated content requires moving beyond simple web checkers and understanding the underlying mechanics of Large Language Models (LLMs), the specific operational risks across key industries, and the technical strategies required to maintain data integrity.

The Technical Mechanics of AI Detection

Automated AI detection tools do not read text for meaning. Instead, they analyze statistical distributions and stylistic patterns left behind by language models. When evaluating text, detection systems primarily measure two core metrics: perplexity and burstiness.

1. Perplexity

Perplexity measures how surprising or unexpected each word in a sequence is to a probability model. Because models like ChatGPT are trained to select the statistically most likely next word, machine-generated text typically exhibits very low perplexity. The phrasing flows smoothly and avoids unusual vocabulary, idioms, or unexpected stylistic turns. This causes AI-generated content to have an almost “robotic” flow to it: you can tell when the writing is almost “too perfect”! Human writing, by contrast, varies significantly due to our usage of a variety of different words, grammar mistakes, and methods for expressing complex ideas via imagery and slang.

  • picnicSurprising

AI tends to pick the top option every time, so its text has low perplexity. People reach for the odd word more often.

2. Burstiness

Burstiness tracks the variance in sentence length, structure, and complexity throughout a document. We naturally alternate between short, punchy statements and longer, complex sentences while AI models tend to produce uniform, metronomic sentence structures with consistent lengths across paragraphs.

While third-party detection software relies on these statistical signatures, modern LLMs have grown sophisticated enough that relying solely on automated detection scores leads to frequent false positives and false negatives. A robust verification strategy requires combining automated checks with domain-specific human oversight.

Industry-Specific Detection Strategies

Different sectors face unique operational risks when AI-generated text enters their workflows. How you detect and verify content must adapt to the stakes of your industry.

Legal: Verifying Citation Authenticity and Precedent

In legal practice, unverified AI content poses direct liability hazards. The primary danger is not simply automated writing, but hallucinated case law where an AI model fabricates plausible-sounding judicial decisions, court citations, or statutory references. This is a dangerous phenomenon known as “hallucinations”.

  • Audit Structural Precedent: AI-generated legal text often presents impeccably formatted legal reasoning wrapped around fake citations. Detection protocols in law firms must mandate that every cited case, docket number, and statutory quote be cross-referenced against primary legal databases (e.g., Westlaw or LexisNexis) before filing.
  • Identify Generic Qualitative Phrases: AI legal drafts frequently default to balanced, non-committal phrasing (e.g., “It is important to consider both perspectives under relevant statutory frameworks”) rather than taking a decisive, client-focused advocate posture.

Finance: Checking Data Lineage and Analytical Depth

In financial services, including investment banking, wealth management, and private equity, raw AI output often lacks deep contextual grounding. Generative models can synthesize public market commentary but struggle with firm-specific nuance and exact numerical reconciliation.

  • Trace Mathematical Calculations: AI models generate text probabilistically rather than running true mathematical logic. When reviewing financial summaries or valuation reports, verify that all totals, percentage changes, and ratio calculations align across tables and narrative text.
  • Look for “Fluff” vs. Specificity: AI-generated financial commentary often uses broad macro summaries without citing granular, proprietary data points (such as specific Q2 capital expenditure line items or regional portfolio yield adjustments).

Education: Evaluating Authentic Voice and Reasoning

In primary, secondary, and higher education, identifying AI content is essential for upholding academic integrity and ensuring students develop foundational critical thinking skills.

  • Compare against Historical Baselines: The most reliable way to detect AI in student work is to compare new submissions against a student’s prior written coursework. A sudden shift in vocabulary, sentence complexity, or tone is a primary indicator of machine assistance.
  • Assess Personal Insight and Synthesis: AI essays excel at summarizing broad topics but struggle to integrate personal class discussions, specific lecture references, or nuanced personal reflections. Prompts that require students to connect theory to specific classroom experiences naturally expose raw AI outputs.

Words of Caution: The Limits of Automated Detectors

While software tools provide helpful initial signals, relying blindly on automated AI detectors introduces significant organizational risk.

Automated detection scores should serve as a signal to initiate human review, never as an automated final verdict.

1. Beware of False Positives

Public AI detectors are known to produce false positives, particularly when scanning highly formal writing, technical documentation, or essays written by non-native English speakers. Because structured, grammatically rigid text shares statistical similarities with low-perplexity AI output, innocent individuals can be unfairly accused of improper AI use.

Did you know?

In a 2023 Stanford study, seven popular AI detectors labeled more than half of a set of essays written by non-native English speakers as AI-generated, even though people had written every one of them.

2. Safeguard Confidential Data

Pasting unredacted corporate documents, client legal files, or student papers into free, third-party web detectors creates severe data privacy vulnerabilities. Many free online tools store submitted text to train their own systems, potentially exposing proprietary intellectual property or violating privacy regulations like FERPA and HIPAA.

Always enforce a human-in-the-loop policy. Automated detection scores should serve as a signal to initiate human review, never as an automated final verdict.

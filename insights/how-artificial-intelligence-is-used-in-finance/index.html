How Artificial Intelligence Is Used in Finance

The three everyday jobs AI now does for financial teams, the verticals feeling it most, and the guardrails that keep client data and figures trustworthy.

In this article

  1. The Three Core Workflows AI Handles for Financial Teams
  2. Verticals Benefiting from AI Integration
  3. Words of Caution: Navigating the Real Risks of Financial AI

The conversation around artificial intelligence in financial services has shifted. A few years ago, adopting AI meant setting up basic automated chatbots or running models in the background. Today, generative AI and custom machine learning models are functioning as active execution layers across modern financial firms.

For CFOs, fund managers, and operational leads, the real value of AI isn’t in generating vague executive summaries. It lies in the technology’s ability to interface directly with massive, fragmented financial datasets to answer precise questions, model scenario analyses, and assemble complex client deliverables in seconds rather than days.

Understanding how artificial intelligence is practically used in finance comes down to looking at the core capabilities powering everyday workflows, the verticals benefiting most, and the critical security guardrails required when deploying these systems.

The Three Core Workflows AI Handles for Financial Teams

At a functional level, enterprise financial AI tools generally fall into three practical operational buckets: data retrieval, running analysis on complex sets of data, and generating deliverables.

  1. 01RetrieveFind one exact figure across every record
  2. 02AnalyzeRun the calculation across several datasets
  3. 03DeliverTurn the numbers into a client-ready chart

1. Pinpointing Specific Data Points Across Fragmented Records

Financial institutions run on mountains of unstructured data spanning PDF quarterlies, LP agreements, audit trails, and disparate ERP systems. Finding a single historical metric traditionally required an analyst to manually dig through multiple filings.

Modern AI platforms act as intelligent search engines across a firm’s internal repository. An analyst can query the system in plain English: Tell me how much the firm invested in private equity assets in Q2 of 2024. Instead of searching file by file, a properly grounded AI engine parses the firm’s private database and returns the exact verified figure alongside a direct link to all source documents in minutes if not seconds.

2. Running Complex Analytical Calculations

Beyond finding static figures, AI model frameworks are increasingly used to perform dynamic computations across distinct financial datasets. Rather than building custom macro spreadsheets for every unique request, teams use AI to run real-time comparative analysis.

For instance, asking a system to run a cost-benefit analysis comparing our total corporate tax expenditures against our net foreign exchange earnings for 2025 requires the engine to pull ledger items from two different accounts, normalize the variables, and calculate the net difference instantly. This turns hours of manual spreadsheet reconciliation into an instant operational insight.

3. Generating Professional, Client-Ready Deliverables

Data analysis is only as useful as the deliverable it produces. One of the highest-ROI applications of artificial intelligence in finance is translating raw data tables into polished visual assets for stakeholders and clients.

When a portfolio manager asks the platform to generate a chart showing how Client X’s portfolio asset allocation shifted between 2025 and 2026, the AI extracts the historical allocation percentages, structures the visual graph, and writes a contextual summary ready for an upcoming review meeting.

Did you know?

The same plain-English request can do all three jobs. “What did we invest in private equity in Q2?” is retrieval. “Compare it with Q2 last year” is analysis. “Chart it for Thursday’s meeting” is a deliverable.

Verticals Benefiting from AI Integration

While every sector within financial services interacts with data, several distinct verticals are seeing transformational efficiency gains from custom AI implementation:

  • Wealth Management

    Financial advisors leverage AI to synthesize client goals, automate customized quarterly portfolio commentary, and draft personalized estate strategy summaries based on real-time market shifts.

  • Risk Management

    Risk officers utilize real-time anomaly detection models to monitor market volatility, stress-test portfolio exposures against macro events, and flag unusual counterparty credit risks.

  • Compliance & Legal

    Compliance teams deploy language models to monitor communications, audit trades against evolving SEC or FINRA regulations, and automate regulatory reporting filings.

  • Trading & Asset Management

    Quantitative desks and execution teams rely on AI algorithms to execute algorithmic trades, parse unstructured earnings call audio, and spot statistical arbitrage opportunities in real time.

  • Accounting & Audit

    Finance departments automate invoice processing, trial balance reconciliations, dynamic audit checklist mapping, and revenue recognition workflows under complex GAAP or IFRS standards.

  • Commercial & Retail Banking

    Banks deploy AI engines to speed up loan underwriting, extract debt service coverage ratios (DSCR) from tax returns, and detect fraudulent transactions before settlement.

  • Insurance & Underwriting

    Carriers use predictive vision and language models to evaluate claims risk, extract details from loss runs, and automate property risk evaluations.

Words of Caution: Navigating the Real Risks of Financial AI

While the benefits of AI are vast, financial firms face unique operational and regulatory risks that make consumer AI tools dangerous if used carelessly.

Security and Data Privacy Are Non-Negotiable

Confidentiality is the cornerstone of finance. Uploading unredacted client financial statements, deal terms, or proprietary fund metrics into public, consumer-facing AI models like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude poses severe regulatory compliance and security hazards. Public models regularly retain input data to train future models, meaning sensitive corporate data could inadvertently leak outside your perimeter. It is possible to build “closed loop” AI systems where financial institutions can use the latest AI models but not have their data sent back outside for models to train on. Additionally, it is possible to build private models that are trained on a firm’s unique documents so that the AI can produce similar documents.

Key term

Closed-loop AI: a setup where a firm uses today’s leading AI models, but its prompts and documents stay inside its own private environment and are never used to train anyone else’s model.

Managing the Threat of AI “Hallucinations”

Large language models operate by predicting statistically plausible language patterns, not by “knowing” factual truth. AI models can fabricate convincing figures, a phenomenon known in tech as a “hallucination”, when they encounter a gap in their context. In financial reporting or trading, an unverified hallucinated metric can lead to severe financial misstatements.

Firms must institute strict “human-in-the-loop” verification rules. AI should be treated as a high-speed research assistant whose outputs are always reviewed, cross-referenced, and signed off on by a qualified professional before being acted upon or sent to clients.
